Side income: the ¥200,000 rule and resident tax

Disclaimer: this page only summarizes general rules published by the National Tax Agency (NTA) and other official bodies. It is not tax advice for any individual's situation and does not file anything for you. For individual cases, ask your tax office (税務署) or a tax accountant (税理士).

Income tax: the ¥200,000 rule

  • One employer: you must file if income other than salary and retirement pay exceeds ¥200,000. Two or more: unadjusted wages plus other income exceeding ¥200,000.
  • The test is 所得 (receipts minus expenses), not 収入. Exception: salary of ¥1.5 million or less after the deduction and side income of ¥200,000 or less may not need filing.

Resident tax: no ¥200,000 exception

  • Income tax filing can be skipped when non-salary income is ¥200,000 or less, but resident tax has no such exception: any non-salary income, however small, must be reported to the ward/city office of your 1 January address (deadline 3/15).
  • If you filed the income tax return, resident tax is treated as reported.
  • Details differ by municipality; ask your ward/city office. (Sources are municipal FAQ pages; we read the search excerpts.)

It can matter for residence renewals

Several residence statuses ask for a 「住民税の課税(又は非課税)証明書及び納税証明書」 (a year's total income and tax payment status, issued by the ward office of your 1 January address) when renewing. Requirements differ by status; follow the Immigration Services Agency's guidance.
